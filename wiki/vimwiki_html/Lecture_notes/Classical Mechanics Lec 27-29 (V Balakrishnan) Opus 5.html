Weiss molecular field theory

The idea is that in the ferromagnetic medium each dipole moment experience not only the ext. field \(\vec{H}\) but also the internal mag. field due to other dipole moments.

\[ H \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{replace}\ }\; H_{\text{eff}} \;=\; H + \lambda\frac{M}{\boxed{N}} \]

\(\downarrow\) to make intensive quantity to add to H.
\[ H_{int} \propto M \qquad\qquad H_{int} = \lambda M \] \[ M \;=\; N\mu\tanh\left(\frac{\mu\left(H + \frac{\lambda M}{N}\right)}{k_BT}\right) \qquad \text{(for 1-D)}. \]

This is transedental equation, we can only solve it by Numerical methods.

Let us see its behaviour near *\(H=0\)*.

\[ M_0 \;=\; N\mu\tanh\left(\frac{\mu\lambda M_0}{N k_B T}\right) \qquad \left(\begin{aligned} M_0 &= \text{magnetisation}\\ &\ \ \text{per unit}\\ &\ \ \text{dipole moments}\end{aligned}\right) \]
  1. \(M_0 = 0\) is always a solution (Paramagnetic solution). \((H=0,\ M=0)\)
  2. But ferromagnet have hysteresis.

*Near \(M\approx 0\)*

\[ \begin{aligned} M_0 &= N\mu\left(\frac{\mu\lambda M_0}{N k_B T}\right)\\ &\approx \frac{N\mu^2\lambda M_0}{N k_B T} \;\approx\; \frac{\mu^2\lambda M_0}{k_B T} \end{aligned} \]

\(\left(\text{at high temp } T' > T_0,\ \text{slope is less}\right)\),
\(\left(\text{for smaller temp } T'' \text{ slope is even higher}\right)\).

\[ \frac{\mu^2\lambda}{k_BT} > 1 \qquad \text{or} \qquad \boxed{\,T < \frac{\mu^2\lambda}{k_B}\,} \]

So \(\left(T = \dfrac{\mu^2\lambda}{k_B}\right)\) is critical point.

The negative solution for \(M_0\) is unstable; so we only have one positive root of \(M_0\). Which leads to ferromagnetism.

So below a certain critical temp, the cooperative tendency of all these magnetic moments to align in same dir. will dominate over disrupting tendency of entropy \((S)\) and we have order.

\[ F = U - TS \qquad\qquad (U >> TS) \]

at low temp \((T)\) \(F \approx U\) (ordered phase) (ferromagnetic)

\[ F = U - TS \] \[ (TS >> U) \]
